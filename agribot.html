Independent Research

AgriBot

An autonomous crop-health rover combining dual-camera NDVI imaging, YOLOv8 detection, and a 4-wheel drivetrain, built to give small-scale farmers an offline, low-cost alternative to commercial multispectral cameras.

Role
Camera + AI model build, mechanical assembly
Status
Subsystems tested, integration pending
Skills & tools

Overview

AgriBot detects crop stress before it's visible to the eye by combining two imaging channels: a standard RGB camera for visible symptoms (discoloration, wilting, lesions) and a DIY near-infrared setup for NDVI (Normalized Difference Vegetation Index), a scientifically validated early-warning signal that commercial multispectral cameras charge $500+ to measure. This version costs roughly $35-45 in cameras and a filter.

The dual-camera head, the YOLOv8 detection model, and the drivetrain motors have each been built and bench-tested independently. The 3D-printed chassis parts are being fabricated separately, with full integration onto the rover still ahead.

How the NDVI channel works

A NoIR camera (IR-filter removed) with a red-pass filter captures near-infrared reflectance, while a second ordinary camera captures the visible red channel. Software aligns the two images and computes NDVI = (NIR − Red) / (NIR + Red) per pixel. Healthy vegetation reflects strongly in NIR while absorbing red light, so a declining NDVI value flags stress before any visible symptom appears.

Model pipeline

The RGB feed runs through a YOLOv8 model for visible symptom detection, while the NDVI computation runs in parallel for early stress signals. Both outputs merge into a 5-category health classification, exported via ONNX to TensorRT and quantized to run in real time, fully offline, on a Jetson Orin Nano Super.

Training the model

YOLOv8n/s is fine-tuned from COCO-pretrained weights rather than trained from scratch, since no dataset exists specifically for Pakistani crop varieties. The training set combines three public sources: PlantVillage (~54,000 labeled images across 14 crop species and 26 diseases), PlantDoc (real-world, field-condition images rather than lab photos), and Kaggle crop-disease sets covering wheat, cotton, rice, and sugarcane. Training targets five general health categories (healthy, nutrient-deficient, diseased, pest-damaged, water-stressed) rather than crop-specific labels, so the same model generalizes across crops before being fine-tuned further as local data becomes available.

Other public datasets worth evaluating for this kind of work: the Cassava Leaf Disease dataset (Kaggle/Makerere AI Lab), the Rice Leaf Disease dataset, and PlantDoc's companion field-image sets. All three share the same limitation as PlantVillage: little to no representation of South Asian growing conditions, which is the gap local data collection is meant to close.

Power & drivetrain

A dual-rail electrical design keeps drive current fully isolated from logic and sensors: a switched LiPo bus feeds two fused motor branches (each behind a blocking diode into a RoboClaw controller) and one compute branch through a boost regulator into the Jetson. The two cameras connect separately, straight into the Jetson's CSI ports, so image data never touches the power wiring at all.
