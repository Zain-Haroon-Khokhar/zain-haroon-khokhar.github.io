System overview

BARQ is an end-to-end student-engineered sounding rocket developed by the Aitchison College Rocketry Team (ACRT) under the Aitchison College Science Society (ACSS). It integrates a custom-fabricated airframe, a wireless remote ignition system, a bespoke dual-redundant flight computer, and a single-parachute recovery mechanism designed to withstand transonic flight loads.

The airframe is optimized for a high strength-to-weight ratio using accessible, cost-effective materials rather than composite construction.

Propulsion & remote ignition

A solid propellant motor (1994K6 35-17A) housed in a custom-machined casing delivers a peak thrust of roughly 72 kg (761 N). Early ignition attempts used a GSM SIM800L module firing over SMS, which proved unreliable due to network latency. The final architecture replaced this with an ESP32 microcontroller running Blynk over WiFi for stable, low-latency remote ignition, firing a nichrome wire igniter through a relay-controlled circuit.

Static ground test

Ground-tested on an HX711 load cell before flight. The curve below shows real logged thrust data: a fast rise to peak around 710 N at 2.5s, tailing off to burnout by 5s, closely matching the motor's rated 761 N max thrust.

The assembled flight computer: ESP32, microSD adapter, dual BMP280s, MOSFET switching, and the Jolly Logic AltimeterTwo backup, all on a single perfboard.

Flight software: state machine

Programmed in C++, the flight computer runs an autonomous state machine that filters out launch noise and accurately detects apogee before firing the deployment charge:

StateTrigger
WAITINGGround state, armed above 2m altitude
BOOST_LOCKOUTLiftoff detected (>0.5m); 5s timer bypasses vibration/pressure spikes
APOGEE_CHECKPeak-hold tracking after boost lockout expires
DEPLOYAltitude drops 2m below peak, confirmed over 10 consecutive samples

Flight results

The flight launched August 11, 2026, and reached a confirmed apogee of 3,740 m at a peak velocity of Mach 0.86, per Aitchison College's official report. The 1,841 m figure below was an earlier RockSim design estimate made before the final "Silver Phantom" motor was selected, so the two numbers describe different stages of the project rather than a discrepancy in the data.

Recovery

A single-chute recovery system decelerates the vehicle after the flight computer's dual-BMP280 peak-hold logic confirms apogee and fires a flare fitting containing powdered fuel. Early trigger-reliability testing used proxy configurations and drone-mounted payload simulations before live integration.
