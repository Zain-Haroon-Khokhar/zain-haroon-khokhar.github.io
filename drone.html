Overview

A quadcopter on a larger off-the-shelf frame, with an ESP32 flight controller, MPU9250 IMU, GPS, and RC transmitter/receiver control link. Zain handled the entire build: assembly, coding, and debugging, solo.

Self-balancing with PID

A quadcopter doesn't stay level on its own; any imbalance in thrust or a wind gust will tip it. The flight controller counters this with a PID (Proportional-Integral-Derivative) loop running hundreds of times per second: the IMU reports current tilt, that's compared to the desired level attitude to get an error, and the PID controller turns that error into a correction. P reacts to how large the error is right now, I catches small persistent drift that P alone won't cancel, and D dampens the response so it doesn't overshoot and oscillate. Motor mixing then applies that correction unevenly across the four motors, for example spinning the front motors faster to pitch the nose back down.

correction = Kp×error + Ki×Σerror + Kd×(error − lastError)

ESCs and motor mixing

Brushless motors can't just be handed a voltage the way a small DC motor can — they need current switched through their three phase windings in the right sequence to keep spinning, which is what each Electronic Speed Controller (ESC) does. The flight controller doesn't talk to the motor at all; it sends each ESC a signal telling it how fast to spin, and the ESC handles the actual phase-switching internally. That signal is the same pulse-width scheme a hobby servo uses — a 1000µs pulse means off/minimum, 2000µs means full throttle, repeated every 20ms:

throttle (%) = (pulseWidthµs − 1000) / 1000 × 100

Motor mixing is what turns one PID correction into four different ESC signals. For a plus-configuration quadcopter, correcting pitch means the front motor and back motor need to move in opposite directions from the hover baseline, while roll correction does the same for left/right, and both mix on top of whatever throttle the pilot has commanded:

frontMotor = baseThrottle + pitchCorrection

backMotor = baseThrottle − pitchCorrection

leftMotor = baseThrottle + rollCorrection

rightMotor = baseThrottle − rollCorrection

Each of those four results gets clamped to the 1000–2000µs range and sent to its ESC every cycle, so the quadcopter is really just running the same PID-to-pulse-width pipeline four times in parallel, once per corner.
