One-Seater Electric Car

A fully driveable EV built from a rickshaw axle and repurposed parts: chain-driven, battery-powered, and manually controlled.

Overview

Built from a rickshaw axle driven by an electric motor via chain-and-gear, powered by 4 car batteries through an off-the-shelf motor controller. Steel body frame, steering-wheel-controlled front wheels, pedal-controlled speed, and a 3-way switch for forward/reverse/neutral. Zain handled the full electrical system; the frame was welded under professional guidance, with Zain learning to weld and working hands-on throughout.

Results

Finished and driveable. Top speed of 32 km/h, runtime of 35-40 minutes per charge.

Power and control

Unlike the smaller Arduino builds elsewhere on this site, there's no microcontroller here deciding direction and speed pin-by-pin — the motor controller is a sealed commercial unit that does that job internally, and the throttle pedal and 3-way switch are just the inputs it reads.

The throttle pedal is a potentiometer: pressing it further changes the voltage on its wiper, and the controller reads that voltage as a request for more speed. Internally it still comes down to the same idea as a smaller H-bridge driver — switching the battery voltage to the motor on and off fast enough that only the average matters, with the fraction of on-time (duty cycle) proportional to how far the pedal is pressed:

duty cycle ∝ Vthrottle → higher pedal voltage = higher average motor voltage = more speed

The 3-way switch handles direction rather than speed: at the two non-neutral positions it reverses which pair of the controller's output terminals sees positive vs. negative, which reverses current flow through the motor windings and flips its spin direction — mechanically the same forward/reverse logic as flipping IN1/IN2 on a small H-bridge, just done with switch contacts carrying real motor current instead of a microcontroller pin carrying logic-level current. Neutral simply leaves the motor circuit open regardless of throttle position, which is what makes it a safe park state.
