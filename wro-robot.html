Overview

Aitchison X is a 4WD autonomous vehicle I built for WRO's Future Engineers category, where robots must navigate an arena, avoid walls, and react to colored obstacles entirely on their own. As electrical lead, I was responsible for the vehicle's entire electrical system: selecting every component, designing the power distribution, wiring and integrating the electronics, testing each subsystem, and writing the navigation control code.

My team won the National Finals in Pakistan and qualified for the WRO International Finals in Turkey.

Electrical scope

  • Selected and sourced all electrical components: motors, sensors, converters, microcontroller, camera
  • Designed a dual-rail power system separating drive power from logic/sensor power
  • Wired and integrated 5 ultrasonic sensors, a Pixy camera, servo steering, and a DC drive motor onto an Arduino Mega
  • Programmed the full navigation and obstacle-avoidance logic
  • Tested and debugged the complete system end to end

How it navigates

Five ultrasonic sensors (front, two at 90°, two at 45°) let the robot detect walls and count laps by tracking 90° turns. A Pixy camera adds color-based obstacle avoidance during the obstacle round: on spotting a green block, it turns right; on red, it turns left. After the turn, it straightens itself to face the next block head-on, then hands back to the ultrasonic sensors, which continuously correct its distance from both walls to keep it centered in the track.

Power architecture

The electrical system runs on two isolated power rails: one for the drive motor, one for logic and sensors, so motor current draw never browns out the microcontroller or sensors.

Sensor & vision wiring

All five ultrasonic sensors and the Pixy camera connect to the Arduino Mega's digital pins, sharing a common 5V/GND bus. The Pixy camera seats directly on the 6-pin ICSP header rather than a numbered digital pin.

Actuator wiring

The L298N motor driver takes ENA/IN1/IN2 signals for the drive motor's speed and direction, while the servo receives its own PWM signal for steering.
